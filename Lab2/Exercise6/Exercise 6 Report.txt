Design Report:
The test suite in Exercise 6 evaluates the symmetric and transitive closure functions 
implemented in Exercises 3 and 5. By importing symClos, trClos, and the custom 
composition operator (@@) directly from their respective modules, the testing logic 
remains cleanly separated from the core algorithmic implementations. Helper 
predicates (isSymmetric, isTransitive, and isSubset) were designed to translate the 
mathematical definitions of closures into strict boolean checks that can be evaluated 
automatically.

Test Report and Property Strength:
The automated test suite leverages QuickCheck to generate random binary relations 
(lists of integer pairs) to comprehensively validate both closure functions against 
three core mathematical axioms.

For the symmetric closure, prop_symClos_isSymmetric ensures the output achieves 
perfect symmetry, prop_symClos_containsOriginal confirms no original data is lost 
(the superset rule), and prop_symClos_idempotent verifies that repeated applications 
yield no further changes. For the transitive closure, the equivalent properties 
(prop_trClos_isTransitive, prop_trClos_containsOriginal, and prop_trClos_idempotent) 
validate transitivity, subset retention, and fixed-point termination.

Regarding property strength, the "specific rule" properties (symmetry or transitivity) 
and the "extensive" properties (superset retention) are strictly incomparable. An 
implementation could trivially satisfy transitivity or symmetry by returning an empty
list (which fails the superset requirement), or it could satisfy the superset 
requirement by simply returning the original list unchanged (which fails transitivity 
and symmetry). Therefore, neither property implies the other, and neither is stronger 
or weaker; they must be evaluated conjunctively to prove the implementation is 
correct. All automated QuickCheck runs passed successfully, confirming the 
implementations satisfy the formal definitions of symmetric and transitive closures 
within the tested domain.
