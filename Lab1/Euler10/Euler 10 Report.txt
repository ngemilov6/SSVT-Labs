Euler Problem 10
Time spent: 130 minutes

Design Report: 
The solution for Euler Problem 10 computes the sum of all prime numbers 
below two million by constructing an infinite, lazily evaluated list of primes. 
To achieve the necessary computational efficiency for such a large upper bound, 
the algorithm employs optimized trial division. Rather than dividing candidate 
numbers by all preceding integers, it restricts divisibility checks strictly to 
previously discovered primes. Furthermore, the implementation utilizes a 
right-fold (foldr) to apply a mathematical short-circuiting condition: the 
recursive division checks terminate immediately once the square of the test 
prime exceeds the candidate number (p*p > n). This bounding mathematically 
guarantees primality while drastically reducing time complexity. To satisfy 
the assignment's explicit requirement for testing bonus exercises, the static 
two million target was abstracted into a generalized helper function, 
sumPrimesBelow, allowing the underlying summation and generation logic to 
be systematically evaluated.

Test Report:
The testing strategy utilizes QuickCheck to validate both the prime generator 
and the summation logic through three targeted properties. The first property, 
prop_isActuallyPrime, ensures the soundness of the highly optimized lazy generator; 
it randomly indexes into the infinite prime list and cross-verifies the extracted 
value against an independently implemented, strictly naive prime checker. The 
second property, prop_monotonicSum, verifies the mathematical monotonicity of 
the summation function by generating pairs of random upper bounds and asserting 
that the prime sum up to the smaller bound never exceeds the sum up to the larger 
bound. Finally, prop_knownBaseCases acts as a deterministic regression test, 
verifying the function against small, manually calculable prime sums (such as 
bounds of 10 and 20) to guarantee that the takeWhile constraint accurately handles 
upper limit exclusions. After successfully passing this rigorous automated suite, 
the function evaluates the ultimate target of two million, yielding the final sum 
of 142913828922.
