1. List of factors that result in invalid IOLTS:
- uncountable set of states Q (e.g. Real Numbers)
- Empty set of states Q = {}
- L_I and/or L_U is uncountable
- L_I and L_U are not disjoint (there is a in L_I s.t. also a in L_U)
- Tau is a label in L_I or L_U
- T is not in Qx(L+t)xQ
- q0 is not in Q

Countability of Q and L_I and L_U are given by the implemnetation, that lists in Haskell are countable by definition.


Definition IOLTS
- Q is a countable, non-empty set of states
- L_I and L_U are countable sets of input labels and output labels, respectively
- L_I and L_U are disjoint
- T in Qx(L+t)xQ is a transition relation
- qo in Q is the initial states
